Home / Solutions / RFID

Item-level accuracy with RFID.

Stock counts, receiving and loss prevention — done at the item level instead of the carton level, and layered on top of the POS platform you already run.

Why RFID

The problems item-level tracking actually solves

Inaccurate stock

Manual counts are infrequent and error-prone, and small inaccuracies compound across hundreds of SKUs and store locations.

Slow inventory counts

Full counts take store teams away from customers for hours — time an RFID handheld scan reduces to minutes.

Overstock & stockouts

Inaccurate inventory leads to markdowns on overstock and lost sales on items that are actually available — just not where the system thinks.

Complex omnichannel

Fast, seamless cross-channel fulfilment depends on inventory data that's accurate in real time — RFID is what makes that data trustworthy.

What's Included

Everything you need, scoped to your rollout

A typical deployment layers RFID hardware and a thin integration on top of your existing POS — no rip-and-replace required.

RFID printer & encoder RFID handheld scanner RFID tags POS & overhead readers (optional) Process design, training & support
Printer · scanner · cloud stock platform
Where It Applies

Tagging through to sales — covered end to end

Tagging

Source, DC or store-level tagging with unique EPC codes generated per unit.

Stock receiving

Handheld scans reconcile received quantities against purchase orders in minutes.

Stock transfers

Inter-store and warehouse transfers recorded and verified by tag, not manual count.

Physical stock takes & sales

Full-store counts in a fraction of the time, with sales and returns reconciled automatically.

Business Value

Why retailers deploy it

Accuracy

Item-level visibility replaces sampled or infrequent manual counts with a stock position you can trust.

Speed

What used to take a store team hours — a full stock take — comes down to minutes with a handheld scanner.

Information access

Real-time stock data feeds directly back into your existing POS and reporting — no separate system to check.

How We Roll It Out

Start small, prove it, then scale

We typically start with a single brand and a small set of stores — confirming tagging standards, store processes and KPIs before wider roll-out.

  1. Preparation — GS1-standard tagging, store process review, KPI baseline.
  2. Trial — deployment and staff training at one brand and store format.
  3. Scale — software enhancement and roll-out to additional brands and stores.
