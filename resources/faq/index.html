FCFootfallCam · 10 questions

FootfallCam is a people-counting solution that measures how many visitors enter your store, how they move through it, and how long they stay. The sensors are installed at entrances and key zones, and the data flows into a central dashboard where you can view traffic by store, by day, or by hour. It turns visitor behaviour into numbers you can act on, rather than guesswork.

Sales data tells you what was bought, but not how many opportunities you missed. Knowing that 500 people entered and 50 purchased gives you a conversion rate, which is a far more useful measure of store performance than sales alone. It also lets you tell the difference between a slow day caused by low traffic and one caused by poor in-store execution.

The core metrics are visitor count, conversion rate, dwell time, peak hours, and repeat visits. Depending on the configuration, you can also measure zone-level traffic, queue length, and staff-to-customer ratios.

FootfallCam uses 3D stereo vision rather than simple beam-break or Wi-Fi tracking, which means it can distinguish between adults and children, exclude staff, and count people walking side by side as separate visitors. Vendor-published accuracy is above 95% under normal retail conditions. In practice, accuracy depends heavily on correct mounting height and positioning, which is why we handle installation planning as part of the deployment.

No. The system counts people as anonymous objects and does not store images or identify individuals. This is an important distinction for compliance, because it means the deployment generally sits outside the scope of personal data collection under Singapore's PDPA, Malaysia's PDPA, Thailand's PDPA, and Indonesia's PDP Law.

Each device requires power and a network connection, typically over PoE, so a single cable handles both. The data is uploaded to the cloud dashboard, so you need internet access at the store, though the devices buffer data locally if the connection drops and sync once it is restored. No on-premise server is needed.

It depends on the width of your entrances and whether you want zone-level analytics as well as entrance counting. A single standard entrance is usually covered by one device, while wide or double-door entrances may need more. We do a site assessment from your floor plans before quoting so the count is accurate rather than estimated.

Integrated Retail handles the deployment across Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, and Indonesia, either directly or through our local partners, so you are dealing with one team across your regional footprint rather than coordinating separate vendors per market.
