Payments Are Now a Growth Strategy

According to the Google–Temasek–Bain e-Conomy SEA report, Southeast Asia’s digital economy is projected to reach $1 trillion in gross merchandise value (GMV) by 2030 . At the same time, digital payments are accelerating rapidly—digital wallets now account for around 50% of global e-commerce transactions.

Yet behind this growth lies a critical challenge: payment fragmentation.

From QR-based payments in Thailand to e-wallet dominance in Indonesia and card-heavy transactions in Singapore, the region presents a highly diverse payment landscape. For retailers expanding across borders, this isn’t just a technical hurdle—it’s a strategic growth constraint.

Payment infrastructure now directly influences revenue, scalability, and customer experience.

 

What is Payment Orchestration?

Payment orchestration is a technology layer that connects multiple payment providers into a single platform, allowing retailers to manage, route, and optimise transactions across markets.

It enables:

  • Intelligent routing to improve approval rates
  • Faster integration of new payment methods
  • Centralised visibility across regions

For retailers operating in Southeast Asia, this is quickly becoming a foundational capability.

 

The Reality of Payment Fragmentation in Southeast Asia

Southeast Asia is not a unified payments market—it’s a mosaic of local preferences, technologies, and regulations.

Diverse Payment Preferences

  • Singapore: Credit cards and contactless payments remain dominant
  • Indonesia: Digital wallets and bank transfers lead due to lower card penetration
  • Thailand: National QR payment systems and real-time transfers are widely adopted

There is no single “default” payment method across the region.

Retailers must localise payment options to remain competitive.

 

The Rise of Alternative Payments

Across Southeast Asia:

  • Digital wallets dominate online transactions
  • Buy Now, Pay Later (BNPL) adoption is rising
  • Real-time payment systems such as PayNow (Singapore), PromptPay (Thailand), and QRIS (Indonesia) are rapidly transforming how consumers pay

In fact, digital wallets account for up to 77% of e-commerce transaction value in Asia-Pacific.

These are no longer optional—they are customer expectations.

 

Regulatory Complexity

Each country introduces unique requirements:

  • Licensing for payment providers
  • Data residency and protection laws
  • Settlement and processing rules

Payment strategy must be adaptable—not static.

 

Why Payment Complexity Becomes a Growth Barrier

As retailers scale across markets, payment complexity compounds quickly.

Checkout Friction = Lost Revenue

Cart abandonment rates can exceed 70% globally—and even reach over 80% in some cases.

If customers cannot pay the way they want, they simply don’t convert.

 

Operational Inefficiencies

Multiple payment providers across markets create:

  • Fragmented reporting
  • Manual reconciliation
  • Vendor management overhead

This slows down operations and increases costs.

 

Reconciliation Challenges

Retailers must manage:

  • Different settlement timelines
  • Currency conversions
  • Disparate reporting formats

Finance teams often spend more time reconciling data than analysing it.

 

Inconsistent Customer Experience

Disjointed payment experiences across online and offline channels erode trust.

Consistency is critical in omnichannel retail environments.

 

The Hidden Cost of Poor Payment Infrastructure

The true cost of payment complexity often remains invisible—until it impacts growth.

Failed Transactions

Poor routing logic and lack of localisation lead to failed payments.

Each failed transaction is a lost sale.

 

Increased Cart Abandonment

In mobile-first markets, friction at checkout significantly increases drop-offs—especially when preferred payment methods are unavailable.

 

Higher Integration Costs

Without a scalable architecture:

  • Each new market requires new integrations
  • Maintenance costs grow exponentially
  • Time-to-market slows down

 

Limited Scalability

Rigid payment systems prevent retailers from:

  • Expanding quickly
  • Adopting new payment methods
  • Responding to market changes

What a Scalable Payment Strategy Looks Like

To scale successfully, retailers must treat payments as a core capability—not an afterthought.

 

Unified Payment Orchestration

A unified payment orchestration layer enables:

  • Integration with multiple providers
  • Intelligent transaction routing
  • Improved approval rates

Retailers adopting payment orchestration for retailers can significantly reduce complexity while improving performance.

 

API-Driven Integrations

Modern payment infrastructure is built on APIs, allowing:

  • Rapid deployment of new payment methods
  • Flexible architecture for scaling
  • Seamless integration with commerce platforms

 

Omnichannel Consistency

Customers expect seamless payment experiences across:

  • Online stores
  • Physical retail locations
  • Mobile apps

Delivering this requires robust omnichannel payment capabilities.

Localisation with Centralised Control

Retailers must balance:

  • Local payment preferences
  • Centralised reporting and governance

This balance is key to profitable regional expansion.

 

The Role of Integration in Simplifying Payments

Payment challenges are rarely isolated—they are deeply tied to system architecture.

 

Connecting Core Retail Systems

Payments must integrate with:

  • POS systems
  • ERP platforms
  • E-commerce platforms

Modern retailers rely on modern POS systems to unify in-store and digital payments.

 

Real-Time Data Synchronisation

Integrated systems provide:

  • Real-time transaction visibility
  • Accurate financial reporting
  • Better operational insights

 

Middleware and API Layers

Middleware enables:

  • Faster onboarding of new payment providers
  • Reduced reliance on legacy systems
  • Greater flexibility across markets

Retailers investing in retail system integration are better equipped to scale efficiently.

 

Key Takeaways

  • Payment fragmentation directly impacts revenue and conversion
  • Localisation is essential in Southeast Asia
  • Integration is the foundation of scalability
  • Payment orchestration reduces complexity and improves performance

 

Future Trends in Retail Payments

The payments landscape continues to evolve rapidly.

 

Embedded Finance

Payments are becoming embedded into retail experiences, unlocking:

  • New revenue streams
  • Seamless checkout journeys

 

AI-Driven Payment Routing

AI is enabling:

  • Smarter transaction routing
  • Higher approval rates
  • Fraud reduction

 

Real-Time Payments and Open Banking

Real-time payment systems across Southeast Asia are accelerating adoption of instant, low-cost transactions, improving both customer experience and cash flow.

 

Tokenisation and Security

Tokenisation is becoming essential for:

  • Data protection
  • Regulatory compliance
  • Customer trust

Retailers adopting omnichannel retail solutions will be best positioned to leverage these innovations.

 

Turning Payment Complexity into Competitive Advantage

Payment complexity is no longer just an operational issue—it is a strategic barrier to growth.

It impacts:

  • Revenue through conversion rates
  • Costs through inefficiencies
  • Customer experience through friction

Retailers that invest in scalable, integrated payment infrastructure gain a measurable competitive advantage.

For organisations expanding across Southeast Asia, a structured assessment of payment architecture—across localisation, integration, and orchestration—can uncover hidden inefficiencies and unlock growth opportunities.

 

FAQs

What is payment orchestration?

Payment orchestration is a platform that connects multiple payment providers, enabling retailers to manage and optimise transactions through a single interface.

Why is payment localisation important in Southeast Asia?

Because payment preferences vary significantly across markets, localisation ensures higher conversion rates and better customer experience.

How do retailers reduce payment failures?

By implementing smart routing, supporting local payment methods, and using reliable payment infrastructure.

What is the role of POS in payment integration?

POS systems act as a central hub for in-store transactions and must integrate with backend systems for consistency and reporting accuracy.

What are omnichannel payments?

Omnichannel payments provide a seamless payment experience across online, offline, and mobile channels.
